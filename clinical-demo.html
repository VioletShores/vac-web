AI-assisted radiology · Diagnostic report sign-off

The model flags a finding on the scan. The radiologist decides whether to report it. VAC seals who signed off — and proves it, years after the read.

It starts in the imaging study: an AI model reviewing the study flags a suspected finding and proposes it be added to the report. A human decides — in the reading room, audited for years. When a model proposes a finding, the clinical and regulatory question is not what the model flagged — it is which registered clinician accepted it, under what reporting privileges, and whether that record has been altered since. VAC is the trust layer on top of the reporting workflow: it proves acceptance, authority, and provenance with a sealed, independently-verifiable receipt.

Illustrative scenario
Meridian Diagnostic Imaging · CT Chest · 09:42 local

A patient's CT chest study is reviewed by an AI reporting-assist model as part of routine triage. The model flags a suspected finding — a solid pulmonary nodule it had not been asked to specifically look for — and proposes wording be added to the report. The finding emerges from that model run: a 6mm solid nodule, right upper lobe, flagged for the reporting radiologist's attention.

The reporting radiologist opens the study, reviews the model's proposal against the images directly, and accepts the finding — in their own wording, not the model's. VAC is the mechanism that seals which study, which model and model version proposed the finding, who accepted it, and at exactly what time — producing a regulator-grade audit receipt that anyone with the JTI can verify against the production backend, without needing to trust VAC's word for it.

The model's flag and proposed wording are automated outputs — no authority is exercised here. Authority begins the moment a registered radiologist reviews the finding against the images and decides to act on it. That is the step VAC seals.

Decision — reporting radiologist reviews and accepts

Human decision · authority not yet verified · record not yet sealed

The reporting radiologist opens the flagged series, reviews the nodule directly against the images — not just the model's summary — and accepts the finding, writing it into the report in their own clinical wording:

Radiologist's accepted wording
"6mm solid nodule, right upper lobe, series 4/image 118. Solitary, low-risk morphology. Recommend low-dose CT follow-up at 12 months per Fleischner criteria for a patient at this risk stratum."

At this point a decision has been made — but no authority has been verified and no record has been sealed. If the acceptance were logged only in the reporting workstation, the audit trail would rest on that system's integrity. VAC replaces that assumption with a cryptographic proof: not just who clicked accept, but who held current registration and reporting privileges for this modality, at this institution, at this moment.

decision: accept, reworded finding: RUL nodule, 6mm identity not yet verified

Authority — VAC verifies registration and reporting privileges

VAC credential gate · policy-encoded conditions · authority chain minted

VAC checks that the reporting radiologist is:

  • Holding current professional registration — active and unrestricted, verified against the credentialing record for this clinician (not asserted by the reporting workstation).
  • Holding reporting privileges for this modality — CT chest reporting privileges at Meridian Diagnostic Imaging, not general registration alone.
  • Acting at this moment — privileges checked live, at the moment of sign-off, not read from a cached credential that may have lapsed since last renewal.

The registration and privileges conditions — modality, institution, credential expiry — travel with the authority chain. Not stored separately in a workstation config that must be trusted; not asserted by the clinician. The part of the system that checks the work is never the part that did the work.

Seal — VAC seals who, which study, which model, and when

Ed25519 signed token · live production backend · independently verifiable

VAC mints a signed token (Ed25519 + JWT) that binds together:

  • Who — the reporting radiologist who accepted the finding, verified against the credentialing record at sign-off
  • Which study — the specific imaging study reported on: CT Chest, Meridian Diagnostic Imaging, accession ILLUSTRATIVE-CT-48213
  • Which model, which version — the AI system that proposed the finding: PulmoNet-CT v4.2.1, and the exact wording it proposed
  • When — the exact timestamp, immutable, signed into the token — queryable in a regulator-grade audit years after the report was filed

The token is signed by the VAC production backend. No party — not the radiologist, not the reporting workstation, not VAC — can alter this record after the fact without the signature failing verification. The model proposes; VAC proves who accepted.

Receipt — a record queryable without trusting the system that wrote it

Live token · verifiable by JTI · tamper-evident

The sealed receipt is queryable by anyone who holds the JTI — the institution, a regulator, or a later reviewer — against the live VAC backend. No trust in VAC required: the Ed25519 signature either verifies or it does not.

What a later review looks like with this receipt: the reviewer queries the JTI — months or years after the report was filed, perhaps in response to a complaint or a peer review. The backend returns the signed token. The reviewer verifies the signature against VAC's public key. The token's claims show the verified radiologist identity, which study was reported on, which model and model version proposed the finding, the registration and privileges that authorised the sign-off, and the immutable timestamp. No internal PACS/RIS access required. No trust chain back to the reporting workstation's own log. The model proposed; the record proves who accepted, and under what authority.

The regulatory backdrop — the EU AI Act and medical devices
AI systems used as a safety component in medical devices, or that are themselves medical devices under Regulation (EU) 2017/745, fall into the EU AI Act's high-risk class via Article 6(1) wherever the device already requires third-party conformity assessment — with the Act's obligations applying from 2 August 2027 for those already-regulated products, and penalties set against worldwide turnover, whichever is higher (up to €15M or 3%).
