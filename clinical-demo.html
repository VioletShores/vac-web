AI-assisted radiology · Diagnostic imaging sign-off

The AI model flags a finding. The radiologist reviews the images and signs. VAC seals who held reporting authority to accept it — and proves it, years after the report is filed.

It starts in the imaging study: an AI model reviewing a scan flags a suspected finding and proposes it for the report, the reporting radiologist reviews that proposal against the actual images, and a human decides — in moments, audited over years. When an AI model proposes adding a finding to a diagnostic report, the clinical and regulatory question is not what the model detected — it is who held reporting authority to accept it, for this modality and this institution, and whether that record has been altered since. VAC is the trust layer on top of the reporting workflow: it proves acceptance, authority, and provenance with a sealed, independently-verifiable receipt.

Illustrative scenario
Westbridge Medical Imaging — Radiology Reporting · 09:42 AEST

A chest CT study (accession WMI-CT-central) is read first by an AI review model, which flags a suspected finding and proposes it be added to the report: an 8mm solid pulmonary nodule in the right upper lobe, flagged at 0.87 confidence. The study is then opened by the reporting radiologist, who reviews the AI's proposal against the actual imaging series — not the model's summary of it.

The radiologist accepts the finding, in their own wording, for inclusion in the signed report. VAC is the mechanism that seals which study, which model and model version proposed the finding, who accepted it, and at exactly what time — producing an audit-grade receipt that the institution, a regulator, or a later reviewer can verify against the production backend, without needing to trust VAC's word for it.

LIVE SEAL MECHANICS

"The part of the system that checks the work is never the part that did the work." The AI model that flagged the finding does not sign the report. The radiologist who signs does not operate the seal. The regulator or later reviewer who audits holds a receipt that neither party can alter after the fact.

2

Decision — reporting radiologist reviews and accepts

Human decision · identity not yet verified · authority not yet sealed

The reporting radiologist opens the study, reviews the AI model's proposed finding against the actual imaging series, and accepts it — in their own wording, for the signed report: "Confirmed. 8mm solid nodule, right upper lobe. Recommend 6-month follow-up CT per Fleischner criteria."

At this point a decision has been made — but no reporting authority has been verified and no record has been sealed. If the acceptance were logged only in the reporting system, the audit trail would rest on that system's integrity. VAC replaces that assumption with a cryptographic proof: not just who clicked accept, but who held current reporting privileges for this modality at this institution, at this moment.

decision: accept finding wording: radiologist's own identity not yet verified
3

Authority — VAC verifies current reporting privileges

VAC credential gate · policy-encoded conditions · authority chain minted

VAC checks that the radiologist is:

  • Holding current professional registration — not lapsed, not suspended, confirmed at the moment of sign-off.
  • Holding reporting privileges for this modality (CT) at this institution (Westbridge Medical Imaging), encoded in the radiologist's VAC credential, not asserted by the reporting system.
  • Acting at this moment — the credential check is performed at the point of acceptance, not read from a stale roster.

The policy conditions — registration status, modality scope, institutional privileges — travel with the authority chain. Not stored separately in a credentialing system that must be trusted; not asserted by the radiologist. The part of the system that checks the work is never the part that did the work.

registration: current privileges: CT reporting, Westbridge checked: at moment of accept policy: embedded in token
4

Seal — VAC seals who, which study, and when

Ed25519 signed token · live production backend · independently verifiable

VAC mints a signed token (Ed25519 + JWT) that binds together:

  • Who — the verified radiologist identity who accepted the finding
  • Which study — the specific accession accepted: WMI-CT-central, chest CT, right upper lobe finding
  • Which model, which version — PulmoNet-CAD v4.2.1, the model that proposed the finding
  • When — the exact timestamp, immutable, signed into the token — queryable in an audit years after the report is filed

The token is signed by the VAC production backend. No party — not the radiologist, not the reporting system, not VAC — can alter this record after the fact without the signature failing verification. The model proposes; VAC proves who accepted.

5

Receipt — audit-grade sealed receipt, queryable after the fact

Live token · verifiable by JTI · tamper-evident

The sealed receipt is queryable by anyone who holds the JTI — the institution, a regulator, or a later reviewer — against the live VAC backend. No trust in VAC required: the Ed25519 signature either verifies or it does not.

What a later review looks like with this receipt: the reviewer queries the JTI — months or years after the report was filed. The backend returns the signed token. The reviewer verifies the signature against VAC's public key. The token's claims show the verified radiologist identity, which study and finding were accepted, which model and model version proposed it, the registration and privilege conditions that authorised the sign-off, and the immutable timestamp. No internal PACS or reporting-system access required. No trust chain back to the reporting log. The model proposed; the record proves who accepted, and under what authority.
