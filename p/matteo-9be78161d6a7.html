Working with AI agents daily made the missing layer impossible to ignore: agents can act, but nothing proves which accountable human authorised the action, within what limits, at that moment. The protocol, in control-engineering terms

VAC — the Verifiable Authority Chain the interlock

A live biometric ceremony roots an agent's authority in a real, present human (face liveness + deepfake scan + a cross-modal challenge: digits spoken AND shown on fingers in the same contiguous recording, so pre-prepared artifacts can't be stitched together). Delegation is bounded and revocable — capability-scoped, like a permit-to-work. Every authorised action emits a tamper-evident sealed receipt: the audit trail exists by construction, not by log-collection.

Athena / L0 packets the control loop

Every AI task travels as a governed packet — identity, policy limits, evidence chain — through a fixed lifecycle (plan → execute → verify → attest). Think of it as the DCS function block for cognitive work: nothing runs outside the loop, and the attestation phase is the loop closing.

SignalRank the calibration regime

Routing across AI models isn't done on vendor benchmarks — every model is continuously scored on known-answer challenges per domain, and routing weight follows the measured evidence, with drift visible. Exactly how you'd treat instruments: never trust the datasheet, calibrate in situ, re-calibrate on schedule. The adaptive risk engine (we call it CoPS/PID — the COPS lineage is literal) then selects verification depth per action: a trusted operator on a routine action gets a streamlined ceremony; a critical action gets the full modality set, floored so trust can never weaken a consequential action.
