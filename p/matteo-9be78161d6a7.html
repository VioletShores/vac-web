The protocol, in control-engineering terms

VAC — the Verifiable Authority Chain the interlock

A live biometric ceremony roots an agent's authority in a real, present human (face liveness + deepfake scan + a cross-modal challenge: digits spoken AND shown on fingers in the same contiguous recording, so pre-prepared artifacts can't be stitched together). Delegation is bounded and revocable — capability-scoped, like a permit-to-work. Every authorised action emits a tamper-evident sealed receipt: the audit trail exists by construction, not by log-collection.

Athena / L0 packets the control loop

Every AI task travels as a governed packet — identity, policy limits, evidence chain — through a fixed lifecycle (plan → execute → verify → attest). Think of it as the DCS function block for cognitive work: nothing runs outside the loop, and the attestation phase is the loop closing.

SignalRank the calibration regime

Routing across AI models isn't done on vendor benchmarks — every model is continuously scored on known-answer challenges per domain, and routing weight follows the measured evidence, with drift visible. Exactly how you'd treat instruments: never trust the datasheet, calibrate in situ, re-calibrate on schedule. The adaptive risk engine (we call it CoPS/PID — the COPS lineage is literal) then selects verification depth per action: a trusted operator on a routine action gets a streamlined ceremony; a critical action gets the full modality set, floored so trust can never weaken a consequential action.

In your language: Zero Trust, extended to agents

You wrote it in your own profile — Zero Trust Architecture, security by design. VAC is that principle carried one layer up: never trust an AI agent's authority, always verify it — back to a live, biometrically-present human, per action class, with a sealed receipt. Identity-first security where the identity is the accountable person behind the agent, not the agent itself.

The market moment

Three forces are converging. Agentic AI is crossing into consequential actions — payments, filings, releases, infrastructure changes — and every one of those actions needs an answer to "who authorised this?" that stands up afterwards. The EU AI Act's enforcement wave turns that from good practice into a budgeted, board-level compliance obligation across every regulated organisation in Europe — and today they answer it with screenshots and log-scraping. No incumbent owns the answer: identity vendors verify humans at login, not per action; observability vendors record what happened, not who was accountable. The gap between those two is exactly where this protocol sits — with a 1,068-claim patent estate and an open standards track staked around it.

Where this is going

Near term: paid design-partner pilots on one irreversible action class — the zero-integration compliance retrofit (a seal-request link plus an evidence page, no code changes on the customer's side) — with financial services and regulated-advice firms first. In parallel: the two Internet-Drafts advancing at the IETF, the patent family building, and the reference implementation hardening in production. The layered end-state: an open standard anyone can implement, a protected reference implementation, regional implementation partners, and a vendor-neutral advisory practice — so when a board asks "who do we buy from?", the answer is clean at every layer.
