The accountability problem

Agentic AI is crossing into consequential actions — payments, filings, releases, infrastructure changes. Every one of those actions needs an answer to who authorised this? that stands up afterwards: to an auditor, a regulator, or a court.

The incumbent answer is screenshots and log-scraping. That is not accountability; it is archaeology. Identity vendors verify humans at login — not per action. Observability vendors record what happened — not who was accountable for it. The gap between those two is exactly what this protocol fills.

The EU AI Act's enforcement wave turns this from good practice into a budgeted, board-level compliance obligation across every regulated organisation in Europe. Article 50 deepfake-disclosure obligations took effect 2 August 2026. Organisations with existing AI deployments must make them accountable — they cannot wait for a rebuild. Accountability was your word at dinner; it was the right word.

Who this is for first

Accountable-decision sectors: legal / tribunal (evidence that survives an auditor), water and critical infrastructure (operational decisions with regulatory weight), and defence-adjacent. The entry is always one irreversible action class — the decision that cannot be undone, that must be auditable, and that currently has no trail beyond a screenshot. Once the first action class is sealed, the authority chain extends naturally across the organisation.

The wedge is the EU AI Act retrofit: a seal-request link and an evidence page, no code changes on the customer's side. An existing AI deployment becomes auditable in an afternoon.

The protocol, in control-engineering terms

VAC — the Verifiable Authority Chain the interlock

BUILT · LIVE

A live biometric ceremony roots an agent's authority in a real, present human: face liveness, deepfake scan, and a cross-modal challenge — digits spoken AND shown on fingers in the same contiguous recording, so pre-prepared artifacts cannot be stitched together. Delegation is bounded and revocable — capability-scoped, like a permit-to-work. Every authorised action emits a tamper-evident sealed receipt: the audit trail exists by construction, not by log-collection.

Assurance proportional to the action the protection relay

BUILT · LIVE

The system reads each action and its context, assesses the risk, and decides how much proof to require — continuously. Viewing a credential might need a fast one-digit re-auth (seconds). Releasing funds or minting authority requires the full modality set. The level is an output of a risk controller (CoPS/PID — the COPS lineage is literal), not a fixed if-then rule.

Athena / L0 packets the control loop

BUILT · LIVE

Every AI task travels as a governed packet — identity, policy limits, evidence chain — through a fixed lifecycle: plan → execute → verify → attest. The DCS function block for cognitive work: nothing runs outside the loop, and the attestation phase is the loop closing.

SignalRank the calibration regime

BUILT · LIVE

Routing across AI models is not done on vendor benchmarks — every model is continuously scored on known-answer challenges per domain, and routing weight follows the measured evidence, with drift visible. Exactly how you would treat instruments: never trust the datasheet, calibrate in situ, re-calibrate on schedule.

Bounded agent delegation scope-limited permits

BUILT · MERGING

An agent can act for a human, but its authority is derived from and bounded by that verified human — scope, expiry, and revocation signed into the chain. Nothing acts in someone's name without tracing back to them. Revoke the human; every downstream agent stops.

Zero Trust, extended to agents

You wrote it in your own profile — Zero Trust Architecture, security by design. VAC is that principle carried one layer up: never trust an AI agent's authority, always verify it — back to a live, biometrically-present human, per action class, with a sealed receipt. Identity-first security where the identity is the accountable person behind the agent, not the agent itself.

On data privacy

Three layers — pick the right rung

Layer 1 (default): Biometric signals are processed in-session and never stored — only the outcome token and the signed receipt are retained. Zero-retention by construction, not by policy promise. Data is scoped to the organisation's tenant from the API layer.

Layer 2 (live, S143): Postgres row-level security enforced at the database layer. A misconfigured query cannot return data outside the tenant's scope.

Layer 3 (by arrangement): SOVEREIGN / dedicated tier — air-gapped where required, never routes data outside the organisation's boundary. For critical infrastructure, defence-adjacent, and regulated operators.

SOC 2 Type II and GDPR Article 25 are planned for Q4 2026 — not claimed today. The architecture is designed to achieve them; the audit has not been performed.
