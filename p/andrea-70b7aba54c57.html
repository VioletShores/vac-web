Verified Human Authority for AI — a Violet Shores × IDSIA program

Walkthrough for Andrea — Sunday 19 July 2026 · Rob Zagarella, Violet Shores

What the business is

AI agents are beginning to take real actions — clinical recommendations, legal decisions, control of infrastructure. Nobody today can prove which accountable human authorised a specific AI action, at the moment it happened, within what limits. Violet Shores has built that layer: a live biometric verification ceremony roots an AI agent's authority in a real, present person; every authorised action is cryptographically sealed with a tamper-evident audit trail; and the whole flow runs on our L0 protocol, where every AI task travels as a packet carrying its own identity, policy limits, and evidence chain. A working system runs end-to-end today; pilot engagements are now being formed. The invention is protected by 1,000+ patent claims across 16 filings, with two Internet-Drafts entering the IETF standards process this month.

The target verticals — specific, and chosen for a reason

Medical.

The vertical where lives are at stake — and therefore where proven human oversight of AI matters most and earns the most credibility. Crack medical, and every other sector follows. Clinical AI needs exactly what the workstreams below produce: proven authority limits, calibrated confidence, and measured human oversight. We see this as the flagship trial domain for the project — and the one where IDSIA's health-AI network is strongest.

The proposed program — "Provably Governed Autonomy"

Research partner: IDSIA. Implementation partner: Violet Shores' Swiss company (incorporating on a positive September outcome). Indicative first project CHF 500–800K over 18–24 months, trials as milestones. Five workstreams shown for breadth — we'd ask Andrea and the panel to choose the lead focus. Each line shows what exists today and what only research unlocks.

Workstream 1 — A provably bounded autonomy governor · Trustworthy Autonomous Systems group (Prof. Flammini)

Today: a working prototype — our policy engine assigns every AI agent an authority envelope (what it may do, at what assurance level), the ceremony gates the action, and each action is sealed into the audit chain. It works, but we can only assert the limits hold. IDSIA research adds: runtime verification and safety-case methods that mathematically demonstrate an agent cannot exceed its envelope — the assurance evidence without which hospitals and utility operators cannot adopt, and with which we become the first certifiable product of this kind. Includes the sensor dimension: physical devices and sensors are treated in our L0 protocol as just another intelligence stream, and how much downstream authority their data may drive should depend on how trustworthy that data is — squarely Prof. Flammini's territory, and squarely the utilities market.

Workstream 2 — Calibrated trust scoring

Today: the system computes live trust scores for every verification and every AI model, from thousands of real challenge rounds — engineered scoring that works in practice. IDSIA research adds: mathematically calibrated uncertainty, so a trust score has a defensible meaning in a courtroom, a clinical audit, or a regulatory review. Market gap: every "trust score" in industry today is a black box; a published, calibrated one becomes the reference.

Workstream 3 — Measuring real human oversight · Responsible AI & Society group

Today: the ceremony puts a verified human in the loop. IDSIA research adds: validated measures of whether oversight is effective — does the human genuinely understand and control what they authorise? The EU AI Act demands effective oversight but no metrics exist; defining them on live trials (strongest in the medical setting) positions this work as the method regulators cite.

Workstream 4 — Context-aware authority decisions

Today: every task in our L0 protocol travels as a packet carrying identity, policy and evidence — but each authority decision is made largely fresh. IDSIA research adds: principled rules for how a decision may use memory and context — the patient's history, prior decisions, the situation — without context becoming an attack surface or a bias engine, and with "what the decision knew" itself auditable. Go-to-market impact: personalised assurance is precisely what medical and financial deployments require and what no one can currently do safely.

Workstream 5 — Independent biometric evaluation & conformance

Today: we deliberately build on existing, proven biometric techniques, and we published our own open scoring methodology — aligned to the relevant NIST and ISO standards — live on our site for anyone to inspect. What we cannot do is certify ourselves. IDSIA research adds: independent evaluation — systematic testing of the verification ceremony against spoofing and presentation attacks, producing third-party evidence of robustness. That evidence is the practical route into production in regulated sectors without waiting years for formal certification schemes to mature.

What launching demands — six research skills, two pillars

The loop: the harness measures, calibration estimates, control governs — and the live system is where the loop closes and becomes provable evidence. To take this to market, six research capabilities are needed that a company alone does not credibly hold:

Pillar 1 — the end-to-end audit chain, launching medical, Switzerland first. (1) Formal assurance of the chain — proofs that seal, delegation narrowing, and tamper-evidence hold, assembled as a safety case a hospital and regulator accept. (2) Oversight-efficacy science — does the ceremony produce genuine informed human control or ritualised approval? Measurable, unmeasured anywhere, publishable — with the live system as the instrument. (3) Privacy-preserving biometric verification in clinical context (FADP/GDPR-grade template handling). (4) Regulatory-science mapping — Swissmedic and clinical governance shaping the launch definition from day one; a Swiss hospital as implementation partner is the natural third leg.

Pillar 2 — intelligence optimisation via SignalRank routing, reliable enough for critical infrastructure. (5a) Harness science — experimental design and metrology for domain known-answer corpora (medicine first: Andrea's steer on the starting vertical), so a harness score is science, not marketing. (5b) Calibration under uncertainty — trust scores with honest error bars (the imprecise-probability lineage); point-estimate trust is exactly what a critical-infrastructure buyer should refuse. And trust is principal-relative: the methodology must support principal-authored weightings — what trust and performance mean for a hospital differs from a defence estate — subjective objectives scored over the same objective measurements. (6a) Optimisation and control — routing as constrained learning governed by a supervisory law with a proven, never-lowered floor. (6b) Standardisation methodology — turning the harness into a conformance standard others can audit and rely on: the bridge from medicine to water, energy, and regulated infrastructure.

Two or three? Twenty or thirty?", citing Sierra's ~20-model architecture, and flagged that with Chinese open-weight models "it is not well understood how eastern-world values are incorporated into the weights." The market is now shipping routers on cost heuristics while openly admitting it cannot answer which model to trust, per domain, on evidence — including cultural-weight provenance. That is precisely the measurement science of WP2. And the capital is arriving one layer up: Salesforce just led a $135M round into 8090's "Software Factory" — governed AI software creation with SDLC audit trails, aimed at regulated industries. That validates enterprise demand for governed AI while leaving the deeper layer unbuilt: authority over decisions rooted in a verified natural person, externally provable — which no funded player occupies, and which is exactly what this research makes reliable. The category is validating itself in public; the science of its trust layer does not exist yet. This project builds it.

Why this end-to-end is a moat no one has

Three well-funded competitor classes each hold one piece and miss the rest:

Identity-verification vendors (the large KYC/onboarding players)

Verify who someone is once, at sign-up. They cannot evidence that a live person authorised a specific AI action at a specific moment within specific limits.

Agent-identity platforms (the big enterprise-software vendors now issuing credentials to AI agents)

Give agents an ID and permissions — but the root is a credential, not a verified living person, and there is no per-action cryptographic seal or oversight evidence.

Attestation protocols (blockchain attestation frameworks)

Publish static badges attached to wallets. A badge can't prove presence, can't bound an action, can't demonstrate oversight.

Only the combination — live verified person → bounded authority → sealed action → provable oversight — answers the question regulators and courts actually ask. That combination is what we've built and what these workstreams make scientifically unassailable.
