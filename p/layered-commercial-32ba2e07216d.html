Layered Commercial Architecture

Violet Shores Pty Ltd · standards / IP / implementation / advisory · updated 3 August 2026 · shared for advisory feedback — please don't circulate
THE QUESTION THIS ANSWERS
When a board procures AI governance, who do they actually buy from? The answer is a deliberate separation of layers so that standards stay vendor-neutral, IP stays protected, implementation stays competitive, and advice stays conflict-free.

The five layers

Layer 1 — STANDARDS FOUNDATION (Geneva, Swiss incorporation targeted)
  Owns:    open standards (AIP protocol, verified-authority specs)
  Sells:   nothing — royalty-free specs, conformance criteria
  Posture: vendor-neutral by design, independent board
                    │ publishes standards
                    ▼
Layer 2 — VIOLET SHORES PTY LTD (Sydney — the IP company)
  Owns:    patent portfolio — 1,068 claims across the combined
           VAC + Athena filings (VAC family closed at 468 claims / 9 filings)
  Owns:    reference implementation (Athena engine, SignalRank,
           verified-authority stack) + product brands
  Sells:   YES — direct cloud product AND IP licensing to Layer 3
                    │ licenses IP, ships reference implementation
                    ▼
Layer 3 — IMPLEMENTATION COMPANIES (multiple, regional + sector)
  Today:   Violet Shores direct commercial (cloud, athenapilot.ai)
  Planned: regional implementers (AU / NZ / UK / US) + third-party
           licensees building compliant software under licence
  Sells:   YES — direct to customers in their region / sector
                    │ deploys software
                    ▼
Layer 4 — CUSTOMER ORGANISATIONS (boards procuring AI governance)
  Buys software from Layer 3 · advisory from Layer 0
  Adopts royalty-free standards from Layer 1

Layer 0 — ADVISORY PRACTICE ("AIP Advisory" — brand locked)
  Standards-anchored, vendor-neutral AI-governance advisory.
  Recommends compliant architecture — never one implementer
  over another (conflict declaration in the engagement letter).

What changed recently

Portfolio scale. The patent estate now stands at 1,068 claims; the VAC family is closed and consolidated at 468 claims across 9 filings — the licensing asset Layer 2 rests on is materially larger than when this architecture was first drawn.

Successor brand. The identity layer is moving to a cleaner public name — Verified Authority — rolled out in gated phases; the layered structure is unchanged, the naming at Layers 1–2 gets simpler.

Runway. The company enters this phase with ~12 months of committed runway, which changes the sequencing question below.

The open question we'd value your view on

Given 12 months of runway, what is the best NEXT commercial step?

The candidate moves, honestly weighed: (a) concentrate entirely on the direct cloud product and land the first paying design-partner on one high-value action class (payments-first); (b) stand up the Layer-0 advisory practice early as a cash-flow and relationship engine; (c) pursue a regional implementation vehicle sooner; (d) prioritise the licensing motion off the closed patent family. The advisory layer was originally conceived partly as early revenue — with runway secured, is it still the right early move, or a distraction from (a)?

And the procurement test: when a board asks "who do we buy from?" — does this structure make the answer clearer or does it read as complexity? Where would you simplify?
