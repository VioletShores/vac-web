Universal message notes

  • Lead with running code. "Working implementation, production pilots, drafts written from what's built" — the most respected sentence in that building.
  • Compose, don't contest. Where does this FIT — never why others fall short.
  • Presence-first. List posts come ~25th, after the meeting, ideally citing these conversations.
  • The ask everywhere is guidance, not adoption. "Where does this belong?" opens doors; "please adopt this" closes them.

Hard zip-it list — whole trip

  • Patents & the Note Well. Formal contributions (mic statements, list posts, presented drafts) covering your patented tech trigger IPR-disclosure obligations. Hallway chat is fine; at the mic, ask questions rather than describing your mechanisms; when the I-Ds are formally presented later, we file disclosures properly. Never say "this is patented" in an IETF room — it reads exactly wrong there.
  • No NXZEN, no exit, no acquirer — to anyone, at all.
  • No customer names — "production pilots" is the ceiling.
  • No production-scale claims — "working system running end-to-end with pilot users," nothing bigger.
  • No standardisation timeline promises — you don't control IETF clocks.
  • Never criticise CHEQ/AGENTPROTO — the community is small and everything gets back.
