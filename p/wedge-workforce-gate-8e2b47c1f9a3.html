The Workforce Gate

Verified-human gating and oversight on every web tool your staff already use — deployed by the organisation via the browser, with zero vendor integration. Per-verification pricing. The shortest line from what exists today to recurring revenue.

The problem — licences buy capability, not accountability

Organisations already pay for the systems their people use. What the licence does not buy: proof of which human acted. 2026 industry reporting is unambiguous — 88% of breaches use stolen credentials; three-quarters of organisations had a SaaS security incident in the last twelve months; over 60% of end-user accounts have MFA disabled or inactive; 58% of organisations say they struggle to enforce access privileges; and one in nine reports a breach traceable to an ex-employee. The May 2026 breach-wave summary put it perfectly: "attackers did not break in; they logged in" — with legal and utilities among the named victim sectors. Passwords and even MFA prove possession of a secret or a device. They never prove a person.

What it is

A managed browser extension, force-installed fleet-wide by the organisation (standard Google Admin / enterprise policy — no user opt-in, no vendor involvement). It watches for the moments the organisation defines — logins to sensitive systems, approval and release actions, payment screens, matter access — and gates them with the VAC ceremony at the policy-set assurance tier. Only a sealed pass lets the action proceed; every event lands as a tamper-evident receipt binding person · tier · application · action · time.

What the organisation gets that the licences don't give them

  • Oversight evidence, per action: "who exercised oversight" becomes a sealed record, not an assertion — the artifact regulators (SMCR, EU AI Act Article 12/14, professional-duty regimes) and courts actually ask for.
  • The shared-credential problem ends: a password can be lent; a live ceremony with continuity matching cannot. The dominant breach vector — the stolen or shared login — stops working on gated moments.
  • Leaver assurance: orphaned accounts stop mattering at gated moments — an ex-employee with a surviving password still isn't the enrolled person.
  • Vendor-audit defence: seat-sharing exposure in licence true-ups becomes measurable and fixable before the vendor's auditor measures it for them.
  • Day-zero deployment: no procurement conversation with any software vendor; the org deploys to its own fleet, Tuesday.

The dashboards — what the admin actually sees

  • Oversight coverage map — which applications and action classes are gated, at which tier, and what percentage of consequential actions carried a verification.
  • Verification stream — every ceremony: who, which app, which action, tier, surface and device, pass/declined — drill-down to the attempt timeline.
  • Security events — continuity mismatches (someone attempting another person's seal), repeated declines, out-of-hours anomalies — each one alertable by email, the channel of record.
  • Shared-credential signals — the same account verified by different enrolled humans across sessions: licence abuse and insider risk, surfaced as data.
  • Leaver report — gated actions attempted against de-enrolled identities: the orphaned-account problem, visible.

All of it rides the surface-telemetry design already specced — one identity, many surfaces, one rail — which is exactly the depth story this product makes visible to a paying customer.

Workflow examples

  • Law firm, matter release: releasing documents from the DMS to an external party is a gated moment at Full tier. The associate's ceremony seals the release; privilege log gains a person-bound entry; a borrowed login can't release anything.
  • Water utility, operational approval: a pressure-reduction change in the SCADA vendor's web console requires a Seal-tier ceremony by a named authorised engineer — the digital twin proposes, a verified human disposes, the receipt proves it.
  • Advice firm under SMCR: sign-off screens in the advice platform gate at Full for the named senior manager — "effective oversight" becomes a per-decision sealed record ready for the FCA's near-continuous supervisory model.
