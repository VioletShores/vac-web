First external user — the onboarding experience

Rob has a candidate first non-Rob user: a senior product and operations person at a fintech, met at the Walmer tennis club, whose stated pain is that leadership is pushing AI adoption with no way to verify or audit the outcome. Her first ten minutes have to demonstrate the argument, not describe it — attribution she can see, limits stated before she trips over them, and one genuinely useful piece of work done in front of her.

The design principle

Everything below follows from one rule: the rails are the pitch, not the apology. The product's entire claim is that governed AI beats ungoverned AI. An onboarding that hides what is unfinished would contradict the claim on first contact. So the constraints get stated up front, in her own professional vocabulary, as evidence that the discipline is real.

The corollary is that we do not ship her a stripped demo pretending to be a product. She gets a small surface that works completely, and an honest map of the edges.

The journey, seven steps

01Invite

Rob sends the one-pager link and the WhatsApp number in person or by message. No app install, no account creation, no password. The channel she already lives in.

02First message

She sends anything. Recognition is by phone number; provisioning happens on first contact against an allowlist, so an unknown number gets a polite decline rather than a session.

03Welcome

One message. Three constraints stated, three capabilities offered, one instruction on how to complain. Copy below — it is the most important artefact on this page.

04First real task

She sends a link — ideally something for her paper. She gets a brief with sources and the model named. This is the moment the argument either lands or does not.

05Her hub

A private page of her own activity: what she asked, what answered, what it cost in confidence terms. The per-user hub route already exists and needs content discipline, not invention.

06Feedback

Plain language in the chat. No form, no rating scale. It routes to Rob and is acknowledged as received, because unacknowledged feedback teaches people to stop giving it.

07Identity seal, optional

Only if she wants it: the verification ceremony roots her as a named human. Never a gate on using the assistant. It is the demo, offered, not the toll gate.

The welcome message

Plain text, no markdown, no emoji — WhatsApp renders neither, and a reply full of hash symbols would undercut the product on the first screen. Roughly 150 words, readable on one phone screen without scrolling twice.

Hello — this is Athena, Rob Zagarella's assistant. He has given you early access. Three things first, because they are the point rather than the small print. One. Every answer tells you which model produced it. When that cannot be determined, it says so instead of quietly leaving it out. Two. Please do not send anything confidential yet. Per-user data isolation is still being finished. I would rather you knew that now than found out later. Three. I do a small number of things well at the moment, not everything. What I can do today: - Send me a link and I will come back with a brief on it, with sources and the model named - Ask me a question and I will route it to whichever model our calibration data rates strongest for that subject, and tell you why it was chosen - Ask what can you do at any time to see this list again If something is wrong or irritating, just say so here. It goes straight to Rob.

Function allowlist, version one

Four capabilities. Anything outside the list gets a specific decline that names what is available, never a generic refusal and never a silent failure.

  • Link to brief — she sends a URL, gets a structured brief with sources and attribution. Her paper research is the obvious first use.
  • Routed question — a question is classified by subject, routed on calibration evidence, answered with the model named and the reason for choosing it.
  • Capability list — asking what it can do returns the current honest list, generated from the allowlist rather than hand-written, so it cannot drift from reality.
  • Feedback — anything phrased as a complaint or suggestion is captured, acknowledged, and surfaced to Rob.

Explicitly out of scope for version one

Dispatching work to build systems, anything touching the repositories, cost and infrastructure surfaces, other people's data, and any storage of confidential material. Not because they are hard, but because each one widens the blast radius before isolation is proven.

Failure behaviour

The failure modes matter more than the happy path here, because she is evaluating whether the discipline is real. Three rules: degrade honestly — if a model is unreachable, say which one and that the answer came from elsewhere; never fabricate attribution — unattributed is a legitimate answer, a guess is not; never go silent — a message that produces nothing is worse than a message that explains why it produced nothing.
