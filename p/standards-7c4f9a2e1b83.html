Standards & architecture

Two Internet-Drafts posted to the IETF, and a short account of how the system they describe is put together.

Internet-Drafts

  • draft-zagarella-verified-human-root — rooting agent delegation in a verified natural person.
  • draft-zagarella-autonomy-governor — bounded, policy-governed autonomy for AI agents.

Architecture

Every chain of delegated authority is rooted in a natural person proven live, not merely authenticated — a verified human root that agents, organisations, and downstream delegations all trace back to. The question of "which person is accountable for this action" is answered at the root, once, rather than reconstructed after the fact.

Above that root sits an autonomy governor: a policy layer that sets how much independent action an agent may take before a human decision is required, expressed as configuration rather than procedure. Assurance floors, action classes, and delegation scope are enforced at the point of decision, so the degree of human oversight is a property the system can demonstrate, not merely describe.

Requests move through trust-weighted orchestration — work is routed and evaluated against calibrated trust rather than a single fixed model or rule set, with the outcome of each request feeding back into how future requests are handled. The result is one continuous rail from a verified person to an accountable decision.

Standards alignment

The system is designed to map to NIST 800-63-4 identity assurance levels, ISO/IEC liveness detection standards, and Articles 12 and 14 of the EU AI Act (record-keeping and human oversight).

Status

A working system running end-to-end; this page describes how the pieces compose.
