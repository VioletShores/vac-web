Provable human authority over AI agents

As organisations hand real decisions to AI, one question decides who they can trust: which verified human authorised this, and can you prove it? VAC answers it end-to-end: a live biometric ceremony verifies a real person at the moment of a decision, capability-scoped delegation passes narrowed authority to agents, and every action lands in a tamper-evident audit chain that resolves back to that person — demonstrated governance, not described governance.

Try the working system on your phone (about two minutes): vacprotocol.org/tribunal-demo

What exists today

A working system running end-to-end with pilot users: live multi-modal verification (face, gesture, spoken challenge — bound together in real time, so cloned voices and replayed video don't pass), delegation and sealed audit in production flow, a developer SDK in design-partner preview, and multi-language operation (Italian live this week). Underneath sits Athena, our multi-LLM orchestration layer with trust-calibrated model routing — the engine that keeps the verification honest and the answers auditable.

Standards & regulatory alignment

Regulation increasingly demands what VAC provides: the EU AI Act's human-oversight and record-keeping articles, UK and Five-Eyes assurance guidance, and NIST's identity-assurance framework all require effective, evidenced human control of AI systems. VAC is designed to align with NIST 800-63-4 assurance levels and ISO/IEC liveness standards, and we're engaged at the protocol level: two Internet-Drafts now posted to the IETF — draft-zagarella-verified-human-root and draft-zagarella-autonomy-governor — with participation at IETF 126 in Vienna this month. The intellectual property position is deep — 1,028+ claims across 16 filings — while the protocol layer is being shaped for open adoption.

The bigger picture

VAC is the trust root of a larger system. Athena, the orchestration layer beneath it, runs every AI task as a structured, auditable unit of work — planned, executed, verified, and sealed — with the model for each task chosen by empirically calibrated trust scores rather than habit or marketing claims. Those scores are ours: we test the major frontier and open-source models — Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI, DeepSeek, Meta and Moonshot families among them — against our own domain-by-domain harnesses (medical, financial, regulatory, strategy, with an execution-verified coding harness in build), so routing decisions rest on evidence we generated, not on published benchmarks. The part I enjoy telling people over lunch: the system is built by itself — every feature that ships, including everything on this page, is dispatched, reviewed, and recorded through that same protocol. The audit trail isn't something we added for customers; it's how the work gets done here every day.

SignalRank — PageRank for the age of agents

Why we built it: the same thing is about to happen to intelligence that happened to information. When URLs proliferated, the web became unusable until PageRank turned link structure into a ranking signal you could trust. Models and agents are now proliferating the same way — thousands of them, wildly uneven, each claiming its own benchmarks — and picking by brand loyalty or marketing is already failing. SignalRank is the ranking layer for that world: an active, continuously calibrated trust graph that tests models against known-answer harnesses domain by domain (11,000+ scored challenges across 32 domains and counting) and routes every piece of work to the model the evidence trusts for that domain. As the population of agents explodes, the durable asset isn’t any one model — it’s the ranking engine that knows, measurably, who to trust with what.

The control loop — COPS & PID

Autonomy without a control system is just hope, so ours is engineered as one — and both names carry real lineage. COPS is the IETF’s Common Open Policy Service (RFC 2748): the internet’s standard architecture for policy control, in which a policy decision point rules on what’s permitted and enforcement points apply it at the edge — a protocol the founder deployed in production carrier networks in an earlier networking career. Our engine carries that exact model into AI authority: the policy engine is the decision point (which actions require which assurance tier, which authority, which budget), and every verification verdict is an enforcement point. PID extends the classic proportional-integral-derivative controller — the workhorse of industrial process control — to a new controlled variable: autonomy itself. Policy bounds are the setpoint; trust scores, outcomes, and audit results are the continuous feedback signal; the controller expands autonomy where the evidence supports it and contracts it the moment it doesn’t — with a human decision gate at every boundary the policy marks as consequential, and every adjustment recorded.

The intellectual property position

The architecture is protected as two patent families filed to date as 1,000+ claims across 16 filings: the VAC family — verified-human authority (multi-modal liveness binding, delegation chains, capability-scoped and sub-delegated authority, sealed attestation); and the Athena family — governed orchestration (trust-calibrated model routing, the packet lifecycle with provenance, policy-governed autonomous operation and resource rails). The protocol layer is being shaped for open adoption on top of that protected core — the same pattern the internet’s foundational companies used: open standards, defensible engine.

Protocols & standards — adopt, extend, create

This is protocol work, and it follows a deliberate ladder. Where strong standards exist, we adopt and align: the identity layer is designed to map to NIST 800-63-4 assurance levels and ISO/IEC liveness standards, and the governance layer to the EU AI Act's human-oversight and record-keeping articles. Where standards stop short, we extend them in the open: nothing today roots AI-agent delegation in a verified natural person, so we've posted two Internet-Drafts to the IETF — verified-human-root and autonomy-governor — positioned to compose with, not compete against, the emerging agent-protocol work (the proposed IETF group on agent audit records and delegation traceability is the natural home). And where no standard exists at all, we're creating one: the L0 protocol — every AI task as a packet carrying its authority, policy, budget, and provenance to a sealed attestation — has no equivalent in any standards body yet. Our approach is the old internet ethos: running code first. The protocol runs our own company daily before we propose it to anyone else, and the intention as it matures is stewardship by an independent foundation — a standard should outlive its inventor.

Where this lands in financial services

The Mills Review frames the sector's central challenge as managing the autonomy spectrum — humans moving from operators to observers of AI acting within boundaries. That spectrum needs an enforcement mechanism, and our assurance tiers are exactly that: an organisation's position on the spectrum, set as policy, enforced per action, changeable from the next request onward. The Review names verification and identity among the prerequisite building blocks for agentic infrastructure — the layer this page describes. Under SMCR, named senior humans remain accountable: a verified-human root with delegation chains is how "who exercised oversight" becomes evidence rather than assertion — the new forms of automated evidence the Review says firms will need to compile and explain. And as the regulator moves toward near-continuous supervisory monitoring, sealed attestation streams are built to be consumed by exactly that kind of supervision.

AthenaPilot — the whole stack in your pocket

The fastest way to feel everything above is the product that runs on it daily: AthenaPilot, delivered where you already live — WhatsApp. No app, no install. You message a number, and every reply rides the entire stack: your verified identity at the root, policy governing what may happen, each request handled as a sealed unit of work, and the answering model chosen per domain by measured trust. It is how we build the system itself — every feature on this page was driven through it — which makes it the most honest demonstration we have. A few of the things the early cohort will experience:

  • Your own memory — it remembers what you tell it, privately and under your control; "forget everything about me" is a command it honours, provably.
  • Answers with receipts — which model answered and why it was trusted for that domain, visible on every reply rather than hidden behind a brand.
  • A morning brief produced while you sleep — always-running sentinel packets that gather, verify, and stamp everything they claim.
  • Feedback that becomes product — say "this bit is confusing" and watch it turn into a tracked change with an outcome you can follow to done.
  • In time, your own calibrated domain — the system learns where your judgment is strongest and tests models against it: the first step toward expertise you could one day share on your terms.
