Measuring when AI can be trusted: a proposed IDSIA × Violet Shores research collaboration

AI systems are taking consequential actions faster than anyone can verify them. The scientific question underneath is unsolved and squarely in IDSIA's territory: how do you measure, per domain, whether a model's output deserves trust — rigorously enough that a regulator, a clinician, or a court can rely on the number? Violet Shores brings a working industrial system and its data; IDSIA brings the scientific rigour that turns it into publishable, fundable research.

Why this is a research question, not just a product

Today, model selection across industry is done by brand and habit. Violet Shores routes work by evidence instead: models are continuously challenged against known-answer sets, scored per knowledge domain, and ranked by calibrated reliability — currently 11,240 scored challenges across the major frontier and open-source model families, with the trust scores driving live routing decisions in production. That corpus and method exist; what they lack is what a company alone cannot supply: formal benchmark design, statistical rigour, peer review, and independence. An academically credible per-domain reliability benchmark would be a genuine contribution — the measurement layer the EU AI Act's human-oversight and record-keeping provisions quietly presuppose but nowhere define.

What each side brings

Violet ShoresThe system and the data

A working end-to-end platform: live biometric verification roots AI authority in an accountable person; every action is sealed into a tamper-evident audit chain; an orchestration layer routes tasks on calibration evidence. Protected by 1,000+ patent claims across 16 filings; two Internet-Drafts posted to the IETF this month. Full access to the calibration corpus and harness for the collaboration.

IDSIAThe rigour and the standing

Benchmark and experimental design, statistical treatment of model reliability and drift, co-authored publication in a first-tier venue (IEEE / NeurIPS-class), and the institutional independence that makes the result citable by regulators and industry rather than dismissible as vendor benchmarking.

Why it fits Swiss funding instruments unusually well

The company-plus-research-institution pairing is precisely the shape Innosuisse exists to fund, and the topic sits inside every current European priority on trustworthy AI — Horizon Europe clusters included. A relevant practical detail: the founder holds EU (Italian) citizenship with full Swiss residence and work rights, and the company's long-term governance vehicle is planned for Geneva — so the industrial partner can be structured wherever the instrument requires, without visa or domicile friction.

What is being asked

One hour in September, when Prof. Danani returns from California. The proposal on the table is deliberately small: a single scoped workstream — design of a per-domain model-reliability benchmark against the existing corpus, with one co-authored paper as the first deliverable and a joint funding application as the second if the collaboration proves itself. No exclusivity, no IP entanglement in the research outputs, and the benchmark method itself published openly — the commercial system consumes the measurement; it does not enclose it.

The offer, in one sentence: an industrial partner with running code, live data, patent-protected freedom to share, and standards-track visibility — looking for the scientific partner who can make the measurement unimpeachable.
