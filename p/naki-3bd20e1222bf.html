Moving fast on AI without losing the ability to prove what happened

Leadership says go faster. The people who own the process know that speed without evidence is just unpriced risk. Both positions are correct, and the argument only ends when you can answer two questions with data rather than opinion: is the AI output actually better than the human baseline here, and can you show, afterwards, who authorised what and on what evidence?

Vibe coding versus vibe engineering

Most AI adoption today is the first thing: prompt, eyeball the answer, ship it, hope. It feels productive because the output arrives fast, and the cost lands later, somewhere no one is measuring. Vibe engineering is the same speed with four things around it — a harness that tests output against known answers, a process the work must pass through, memory so the system accumulates context instead of restarting every time, and quantifiable output so improvement is a number, not a feeling.

The operating rule that falls out of it: if you cannot demonstrate the AI result beats the human baseline for that specific kind of work, the human stays in the loop. That is not caution. It is pricing.

Your existing process is the control environment, not the obstacle

The instinct to protect established workflow is usually treated as resistance to change. It is the opposite. Those steps are where approval, segregation of duties and reviewability actually live. AI adoption that routes around them does not remove the control — it removes the evidence that the control was applied, which is a far worse position to be in when someone asks you to reconstruct a decision six months later.

The useful move is to instrument the process rather than replace it: keep the approval gate, but make the approval provable.

Three controls that can go in now

01 · MeasureGround-truth calibration

Score models against known-answer sets, domain by domain, and route work by that evidence instead of by brand or habit. It turns "which AI should we use" from a procurement opinion into a measurement — and gives you a defensible answer when asked why.

02 · AuthoriseA named, verified human

Consequential actions get a real person attached — verified live at the moment of the decision, not a shared login or an inherited session. Accountability that resolves to an individual is exactly what financial-services regimes already expect of you.

03 · RecordA tamper-evident receipt

Each decision leaves a sealed record: who approved, under what authority, on what evidence, at what time, and how much autonomy the system was operating under. A log you have to trust is not evidence. A record that cannot be quietly edited is.

Where this lands with the regimes you will face

This is not a compliance product, but it produces the artefacts the rules are converging on. The EU AI Act's human-oversight and record-keeping provisions ask for effective, evidenced human control and retained logs. Senior-manager accountability regimes in UK financial services ask who was responsible for a given outcome. ISO/IEC 42001 asks for a management system you can audit. Each of them wants running evidence rather than a written policy, and the three controls above are designed to map onto those requirements directly.

What exists today

A working system running end-to-end, not a slide deck. Live multi-modal verification binds face, gesture and a spoken challenge together in real time, so replayed video and cloned voice do not pass. Delegation narrows authority when a human hands work to an agent. Every sealed action lands in an audit chain that resolves back to the verified person. Underneath sits our orchestration layer, which routes each task to a model chosen by calibration data we generated ourselves — currently 11,240 scored challenges across the major frontier and open-source families.

On the standards side, two Internet-Drafts are posted with the IETF — verified human root and autonomy governor — because the identity-of-the-human layer beneath agent protocols is missing, and it is better built in the open.

For the paper

If it is useful, you are welcome to draw on any of the underlying method: how the calibration harnesses are built and scored, why routing is treated as a ranking problem rather than a preference, and the control-loop lineage behind it — the policy architecture comes from an IETF standard for network policy control, and the autonomy controller is a proportional-integral-derivative loop borrowed from industrial process control, with autonomy itself as the controlled variable. Happy to be a source, to review a draft, or to stay entirely out of it and just answer questions.

Early access, deliberately narrow

There is an assistant built on all of this that I use daily to run the business. The offer is to put you on it early — with rails, and with the reasons for the rails stated plainly, since that is the entire argument above. Initially that means a small set of functions rather than the full surface, no confidential material until per-user data isolation work is finished, and model attribution visible on every answer, so you can see which system produced what and why. Being able to say honestly what is not ready yet is the point, not an apology for it.
