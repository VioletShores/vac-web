The one question the call must answer

What exactly would we be founding? Everything else — equity, IP, your time, which hemisphere — falls out of which of two shapes they're imagining. The call's job is discovery, not decision.

The two shapes

Shape A — you fold in: the tech and you become the new company's core. Fastest to a fundable story; caps a general-purpose protocol inside one vertical, entangles the patent estate, makes the foundation ambition someone else's line item. Heart-check: this is the version that felt conflicting.

Shape B — they build on the platform: Violet Shores keeps protocol, patents, roadmap; NewCo is the assurance application and first great channel — licensee, possibly JV. Cisco-as-first-customer works identically. Your Europe anchor exists without surrendering the deep layer.

Elizabeth's "in the tent" point — taken seriously, then answered

She's right about the instinct: investors resist a company whose crown-jewel IP sits in a founder-controlled pocket outside the cap table — a brand holdco is easy; core-tech IP outside reads as hold-up risk in diligence. But the counter-frame matters: nobody asks Twilio to put its IP inside every startup built on Twilio. The question investors actually price is whether the platform is genuine third-party infrastructure with independent life or the company's own core dressed as external. The more Violet Shores has its own customers, revenue, standards presence, and roadmap — the Workforce Gate wedge is doing double duty here — the more credible the platform-vendor framing becomes, and the more a robust arms-length licence satisfies the tent.

  • Middle structures exist (Caroline's territory): exclusive field-of-use licence for the assurance vertical, performance-conditioned; NewCo option to acquire field-specific IP on milestones; application-layer IP contributed while the protocol layer stays out; the protocol's foundation-stewardship destiny framed Linux-Foundation-style — neutrality as an adoption asset investors can like.
  • Stage changes the answer: angels/seed flex; growth VCs harden; a strategic like Cisco cares most about freedom-to-operate and not feeding a competitor — different tent entirely.

Questions for the call (discover, don't decide)

  • What does the company do in their heads — build a product, or deliver assurance on infrastructure? (Shape detector.)
  • Who sells for the first two years while Jack vests? Is Elizabeth full-time?
  • What would Cisco-as-first-customer actually buy — and from which entity?
  • What does Elizabeth imagine the cap table and your time commitment look like?
  • Their view on IP: in the tent, licensed, or hybrid? (Plant Shape B by describing the platform's independent traction.)
