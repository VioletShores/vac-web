Protocol

How the Verified Authority Chain works

Four ideas carry the whole design: a signed token rooted in a verified person, narrowing at every delegation, revocation that cascades, and assurance levels a verifier can require.

1 · The token

A signed statement of human authority

A Verified Authority Token (VAT) is a JWT-compatible token signed with Ed25519.

The root token is issued when a person completes verification. It records:

  • a hashed reference to the verified person, never biometric data;
  • the assurance level the person was verified at;
  • the authorised scope: resources, actions, exclusions and constraints such as a spend limit;
  • a trust score, the delegation depth and the parent token;
  • an issue time and an expiry.

Every action an agent takes can be recorded against its token as an Authority Action Record. A single provenance reference follows a workflow from the root person through every agent, so one query reconstructs who authorised the chain and what each agent did.

The draft Verified Human Root Attestation specifies the root structure so that other token formats can carry it too.

2 · Narrowing

Authority only ever narrows

No agent can hold more authority than the agent or person that delegated to it.

Scope

A child token’s scope must be a subset of its parent’s. Wider requests are refused at derivation.

Trust

Trust can stay the same or fall with each hop. It never rises.

Time

A child inherits its parent’s expiry and can only shorten it.

Depth

Delegation depth is bounded. The issuer sets the maximum for each chain.

3 · Revocation

Revoke the root, stop the chain

Revoking a token invalidates every token derived from it, across every chain.

Verification walks the chain to the root each time, so a revoked or expired hop anywhere above an action makes that action fail verification. In the reference service, revocation is an authorised operator action, not a public endpoint.

Sensitive actions can also ask the root person to verify again before they proceed, rather than relying on an old session.

4 · Verification levels

L1, L2, L3: how strongly the person was verified

LevelHow the root is establishedTypical use
L1API key plus developer identity. No biometrics.Development, testing, low-risk automation.
L2OIDC or enterprise SSO, or device attestation.Workforce agents inside an organisation’s identity system.
L3Multi-modal biometric verification in the VAC ceremony.Consequential actions where the person must be proven present.
A chain is only as strong as its weakest hop. A verifier can require a level, and the check runs against the lowest level anywhere in the chain, not just the root.
Levels are earned, not claimed. The level comes from a verification the server itself observed. Anything unverified or merely asserted by a client falls back to L1.
Where it sits

Four layers of verified authority

Layer 1

Identity

Verifying the person at the root: face, voice, gesture and liveness checks bound in one act, with continuous trust scoring rather than a single yes or no.

Layer 2

Delegation

Tokens derived hop by hop under the narrowing rules, inside and across organisations.

Layer 3

Provenance

Tamper-evident records of every authorised action, with jurisdiction and verification level kept at each hop.

Layer 4

Governance

Group and multi-party authority: M-of-N approval, community-scoped consent, and authority over physical systems.

These layers describe the protocol design. Not all of it runs in the reference service yet; what does run is on Try it.

Scope

What VAC is, and what it is not

VAC adds

A verified natural person at the head of the chain, delegation that only narrows, and receipts a third party can check without trusting the operator.

VAC does not replace

OAuth, GNAP, workload identity or passkeys. Passkeys prove possession of a device; VAC records the person, the authority they granted and the evidence. It is designed to compose with these.

VAC Protocol

The Verified Authority Chain: verified human authority, carried through agent delegation, checkable by anyone.
