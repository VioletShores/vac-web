VAC proves the officer is present and authorised (live biometric + delegation record), that the AI output was advisory and not the decision, and that a specific, accountable human made the call — a sealed receipt that binds authority, identity, and decision in one tamper-evident record.

The core accountability gap: when an AI credit scoring model recommends approval and an officer acts on it, the record usually captures the outcome — not whether the model's recommendation was reviewed, whether the officer formed an independent judgment, or whether the amount fell within that officer's delegated lending mandate. That gap matters when a loan defaults and credit policy compliance is audited, when a regulator examines whether AI-driven decisions were subject to meaningful human oversight, or when a lending institution needs to demonstrate that its credit process meets governance and accountability expectations.

Why governance frameworks are pressing on this now

FINMA Guidance 08/2024 on Artificial Intelligence is explicit: Swiss supervised institutions using AI in decision-relevant processes must maintain meaningful human oversight and a clear audit trail of who made what decision under which authority. The guidance specifically calls out credit assessment and scoring as a domain where AI-driven processes require documented human accountability — not AI-as-endorser, but AI-as-input with a human as the accountable decision-maker.

At EU level, the AI Act classifies creditworthiness assessment as a high-risk application (Annex III); Article 14 mandates effective human oversight and requires that the natural person exercising oversight understands the AI system's outputs and can override them. Where EU law applies — including for cross-border EU operations — the authority trail VAC produces is relevant to demonstrating that human oversight obligation was met. DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act) adds a further layer for EU financial entities: third-party AI tools used in lending workflows are in scope for ICT risk management and operational continuity requirements.

VAC's contribution is narrow and specific: a verified credit officer, a decision documented as explicitly independent of the AI scoring recommendation, a sealed cryptographic receipt binding the officer's delegated mandate to their identity and their actual decision — and an audit trail that makes the full chain answerable, long after the credit moment has passed.

Careful framing: this demonstration shows an auditable human-authorisation pattern that may support governance, oversight and accountability controls aligned to expectations such as FINMA Guidance 08/2024 on AI, and relevant to EU AI Act high-risk creditworthiness assessment where applicable (Art. 14 human oversight). It is not a compliance tool, not certified, and not regulator-approved. No VAC seal constitutes a guarantee of legal compliance.

VAC biometric step-up — verified credit officer

High-stakes decision gate · live biometric · delegated authority bound to identity

Before the credit decision is sealed, the Authorised Credit Officer must be verified as a live, present human — ruling out delegated data entry, AI-generated sign-off, or unattended authorisation. This is the binding that makes the delegation provable: the officer's delegated authority, their verified identity, and their independent decision are sealed together in a single tamper-evident record.

decision_class=high_stakesbiometric_required=trueseal_gate=officer_verifiedstep_up=full_modal

In a live deployment, the step-up threshold is policy-driven. A routine documentation task may require only the institution's standard SSO. A credit decision — particularly one at or near the officer's delegated limit, or involving a policy exception — triggers the biometric gate. The VAC step-up confirms: this specific human, alive, at this moment, exercised this delegated authority. That confirmation is the cryptographic root of the sealed receipt.

The biometric proves WHO sealed the decision. The authority to make that decision comes from the institution's delegation framework — the mandate, the credit policy version, and the officer's role. Neither element substitutes for the other; both are required and both are sealed.

The biometric step-up (face liveness, deepfake scan, cross-modal gesture and voice challenge) is the same one used in the VAC production backend for all high-stakes seal events. Registration numbers are asserted by the officer for demo purposes; in a live deployment they are managed by the institution's entitlement and role management system.

End-to-end architecture — credit AI governance

In order, end to end:

  • A credit application is received and an AI credit scoring module produces an advisory recommendation only. The AI output is never the credit decision.
  • An Authorised Credit Officer reviews the complete evidence bundle independently. The AI recommendation is one input; the officer's independent judgment is the governing step. No automated path from AI recommendation to credit outcome — the officer gate is mandatory.
  • A delegated authority check confirms whether the amount falls within the officer's bounded lending mandate or requires escalation to dual-control or credit committee. The mandate version is locked to the decision record.
  • The officer selects a decision path (four available) and reason codes — both sealed into the same record as the AI advisory output, explicitly separated from it.
  • A VAC biometric step-up confirms a live, present officer — not a bot, a delegated entry, or an AI-generated sign-off. The biometric is the cryptographic root of the sealed receipt.
  • A sealed, independently-verifiable receipt (Ed25519) records who decided, under what mandate, what was decided, what the AI recommended, and the reason codes — answerable to a credit committee, regulator, or auditor years later.

The same authority chain, other credit and governance contexts

Verified officer authority, an explicit separation of AI advisory and human decision, and a sealed audit trail are not specific to SME working capital. The same primitives apply wherever who made the credit call — and that it was independent of the AI — has to be provable after the fact.

Retail mortgage — AI pre-qualification
An AI pre-qualification model produces a serviceability estimate. A credit officer independently assesses the full application — income verification, liability position, property valuation. The officer's decision is sealed separately from the model output, with the mandate version and reason codes bound to the receipt for responsible lending audit.
Trade finance — documentary credit authority
An AI document checking module flags exceptions in a letter-of-credit presentation. A trade finance officer independently reviews the documents, makes an accept/refuse/waive call within their delegated trade authority, and seals the decision with a verified identity record — the audit trail for UCP 600 compliance and correspondent bank disputes.
Credit committee escalation — dual-control
An application exceeds the referring officer's single-signatory limit. VAC routes to a two-officer sealed approval: each officer verifies biometrically, each decision is sealed independently, and the dual-authority record is generated from both seals — a tamper-evident trail for large-exposure governance and board credit policy compliance.
Legal & tribunal — verified authority chain
The same VAC primitives that seal credit decisions seal legal authorities: trust distributions, estate administration, power of attorney attestation.
