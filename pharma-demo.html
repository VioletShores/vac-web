Pharmaceutical manufacturing · Qualified Person certification

A batch record review flags a deviation. VAC seals who held Qualified Person authority to certify the release — and proves it, years after the moment has passed.

It starts in the batch record: a deviation on a finished-product lot surfaces during routine review, and a human decides whether the batch can be certified for release. When a Qualified Person certifies a batch under EU GMP Annex 16, the inspection question is not what the batch record shows — it is who held current QP authorisation to certify this product category at this site, at this moment, and whether that record has been altered since. VAC is the trust layer under the certification: it proves authority, identity, and provenance with a sealed, independently-verifiable receipt.

During routine batch record review for Lot SX-2291-B, the site's Quality Unit flags a deviation: an in-process bioburden hold-time excursion, investigated and closed as minor with no impact on product sterility assurance. The Qualified Person reviews the deviation report and the full batch record — process parameters, in-process controls, the investigation and its closure rationale — against the release specification.

The QP decides to certify the batch for release under EU GMP Annex 16. VAC is the mechanism that seals who certified, which lot, which deviation, and at exactly what time — producing a regulator-grade audit receipt that an inspector can verify against the production backend, without needing to trust VAC's word for it.

Decision — Qualified Person reviews and decides to certify

Human decision · identity not yet verified · authority not yet sealed

The Qualified Person reviews the deviation report and the supporting batch record for Lot SX-2291-B — the process parameters, the in-process controls, the investigation into the hold-time excursion, and its closure rationale — against the release specification and the site's Product Quality Review requirements.

The QP decides to certify the batch for release under EU GMP Annex 16. At this point a decision has been made — but no authority has been verified and no record has been sealed. If the certification were logged only in the site's quality system, the audit trail would rest on that system's integrity. VAC replaces that assumption with a cryptographic proof: not just who clicked certify, but who held current QP authorisation to certify this product category at this site, at this moment.

3

Authority — VAC verifies current QP authorisation

VAC identity gate · policy-encoded conditions · authority chain minted

VAC checks that the certifying individual is:

  • A verified, present human — not a bot, not a replay, confirmed by live biometric at the moment of certification.
  • Holding current Qualified Person authorisation for this product category at this site (encoded in the QP's VAC credential, not asserted by the site quality system).
  • Acting within permitted conditions — product category, site, and certification scope all within the encoded QP authorisation for this batch class.

The authorisation conditions — product category, site, QP scope — travel with the authority chain. Not stored separately in a quality system that must be trusted; not asserted by the QP. The part of the system that checks the work is never the part that did the work.

identity: live biometric authority: current QP status conditions: within bounds policy: embedded in token
4

Seal — VAC seals who, what lot, and when

Ed25519 signed token · live production backend · independently verifiable

VAC mints a signed token (Ed25519 + JWT) that binds together:

  • Who — the verified Qualified Person identity (biometrically confirmed at the moment of certification, not just account-authenticated)
  • Which lot — the specific batch certified: Lot SX-2291-B, finished-product sterile injectable
  • Which deviation — the bioburden hold-time excursion, its investigation, and closure rationale as reviewed at the moment of certification
  • When — the exact timestamp, immutable, signed into the token — queryable in a regulator inspection years after the event

The token is signed by the VAC production backend. No party — not the QP, not the site quality system, not VAC — can alter this record after the fact without the signature failing verification. The batch record documents what happened; VAC proves who certified it and under what authority.

The sealed receipt is queryable by anyone who holds the JTI — a regulatory inspector, an auditor, the site's quality assurance team — against the live VAC backend. No trust in VAC required: the Ed25519 signature either verifies or it does not.

The regulatory backdrop — EU GMP Annex 16 and Qualified Person certification
Annex 16 sets out the QP's personal responsibility for certifying that each batch has been manufactured and checked in compliance with EU GMP and the marketing authorisation, before it is released. A QP's certification decision is a named, individually accountable act — and inspectors expect that accountability to be provable, not merely asserted in a quality system record. Here is how a sealed-certification mechanism maps to that requirement — and it retrofits: the quality system and batch record process stay exactly as they are, and provable QP authority adds at the certification point through simple APIs.
