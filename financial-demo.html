A briefing for regulated financial services

An AI system recommends the advice. A named, accountable senior manager verifies it live — and a sealed receipt proves it, long after the moment has passed.

One accountable senior manager, verified live — at the moment the advice is released.

VAC proves the approver is the named accountable person (identity), genuinely present (live biometric), and exactly where on the autonomy spectrum the release happened (an immutable audit) — an SSO session or a ticked workflow box can prove none of that.

The core value for a firm: an AI advisory system that proposes, never executes, and a bounded, provable hand-off of the release decision to the specific person who carries personal accountability for it. The live biometric is the high-assurance step-up at the moment that matters most: releasing the advice to the client. Every scenario on this page turns on a question a regulator may have to answer years later: did the named accountable person actually authorise this release — verified, live — and can anyone check, independently, that the record has not been altered since?

Why this is breaking now

AI-generated recommendations make the gap between "the system suggested it" and "a named, accountable person authorised it" impossible to paper over. The Mills Review's framing of an autonomy spectrum — running from an AI that only assists a human operator through to a system left to run with minimal oversight as an observer — treats verification and identity as a prerequisite for that spectrum to mean anything in practice, not a compliance step bolted on after a model already ships recommendations to clients. Every seal on this page is that prerequisite made concrete: a verified, named person, a deliberate act, and a signed record of exactly where on the spectrum the decision happened.

Under the Senior Managers & Certification Regime, an accountable senior manager already carries personal responsibility for the outcome — but the trail meant to prove they actually reviewed a specific, AI-generated recommendation is usually an email thread, a ticked box in a workflow tool, or a login that proves a session existed, not that a specific human authorised a specific action at a specific moment. The Mills Review's call for new forms of automated evidence is exactly this: a cryptographically sealed receipt, tied to a live biometric check, that can answer a regulator's question years later — who reviewed this recommendation, under what assurance, and where the system sat on the autonomy spectrum when it did.

The scenario on this page does not itself decide what is suitable advice — that judgment, and the liability that comes with it, stays with the named accountable senior manager. What changes is whether the record of that judgment is provable, or merely asserted.

End-to-end architecture

In order, end to end:

  • A verified, present human is the root — several signals bound together in one act, not a single detector a deepfake can fool.
  • Assurance is proportional to the action — decided, not hardcoded. A risk controller reads each action and decides how much proof it needs: a fast one-digit re-auth to view the sealed receipt, the full check to release advice to a client.
  • The action is gated — refused without fresh, owner-bound re-auth — and the decision is audited: every action and every refusal is written to an immutable record.
  • Each authority decision is a signed, independently-verifiable receipt (Ed25519) anyone can check against the backend — no trust in us required.
