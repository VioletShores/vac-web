Your verified self, on every page you read.

The AthenaPilot browser extension puts the same governed intelligence you talk to on WhatsApp into a sidebar on any webpage — with your identity proven, your trust graph growing with every decision, and every action sealed into a receipt.

Design locked · backend in active build · pilot follows WhatsApp cohort

What it does

Seven capabilities, one principle: the browser becomes a trusted surface of one continuous system — not a separate app with its own memory and its own rules.

AAsk about the page you're on

The sidebar sees the page's title, address, and whatever text you select — nothing more — and answers in that context. Summarise a contract clause, sanity-check a claim, draft the reply.

BOne memory across every surface

The extension reads from the same continuously-built picture of you that WhatsApp does. It knows your projects, preferences, and history from the first message — and everything you do here is remembered everywhere.

CProven identity, not a password

The browser never asserts who you are. It borrows trust from a surface that has already verified you, and every action it takes is bound to that verified identity.

DSign in to any site, in one click

The paired browser carries your verified credential. Any website that accepts it can be approved from the sidebar — no new password, no re-verification, and the site receives a standard token it already understands.

EGoverned model routing

You never choose a model. Live trust scores select the best one for each request inside policy rails you control — and the sidebar tells you what was chosen and why.

FReceipts for everything

Each answer carries a sealed record: who asked, what context was used, which model responded, what adapted. Accountability travels with the work, not with the infrastructure.

GTrust that compounds

Every verified interaction is an edge in your trust graph. The more you use it — and the more surfaces, sites, and people that vouch for you — the more your verified identity is worth.

The experience, end to end

The pairing moment is the part worth pausing on: a surface that already knows you vouches for the one that doesn't. No new account, no new password — trust is extended, never re-created.

Install and open the sidebar

One click from the Chrome toolbar. The sidebar opens beside any page — dark, quiet, out of the way of what you're reading.

The sidebar shows a short pairing code

The extension doesn't ask who you are — it can't be trusted to know yet. It displays a six-character code that expires in five minutes and works exactly once.

You send the code to Athena on WhatsApp

WhatsApp is the surface that has already verified you. By sending the code from there, your verified identity vouches for this specific browser — and the pairing is bound to it, so an overheard code is useless anywhere else. The vouching event itself becomes an edge in your trust graph.

Athena · WhatsApp — "That code matches a Chrome browser on a Mac, requested 40 seconds ago. Pair it to your account? Reply YES to approve."

The sidebar unlocks — as you

The browser receives its own scoped, revocable credential. From now on the system derives who you are and what surface you're on from that credential — the browser is never asked, and never trusted, to say so itself.

Ask, in the context of the page

Select a paragraph, ask a question. The answer arrives with a light narration of what the system did on your behalf — which model was chosen and why, what page context was used, whether the format adapted to the surface.

Sidebar — "Answered with the model currently ranked highest for legal analysis at comparable trust and a fraction of the cost. Page context: your selected clause only. Sealed to receipt."

Sign in to a site — without a password

A site asks you to verify. The sidebar shows exactly what the site will receive, you approve in one click, and the site gets a standard sign-in token backed by your biometric root. Another verified event; another edge in your graph.

Walk away — the conversation follows you

Close the laptop. On the train, WhatsApp can pick up the same thread: what you read, what you asked, what you approved. If you ever want to cut the browser loose, one message revokes its credential instantly.

Your trust graph — identity that compounds

Most systems treat identity as a gate you pass once. Here it's an asset you build. Every verified action — a pairing approved, a decision confirmed, a site signed into, a person or system that vouches for you — is recorded as an edge in your personal trust graph, anchored to a live biometric root.

Vouching and references work both ways — and not only between humans. A verified surface vouches for a new one; a colleague's verified identity can reference yours; and the agents you operate — software today, embodied robots tomorrow — accumulate their own provable history of acting correctly under your authority, strengthening your graph with every governed action. Trust stops being a claim and becomes a record.

You can always see your position. The product is designed to show every user a live visual of their trust graph — the strength of their root, the freshness of their verifications, the reach of their references — and exactly which action would strengthen it next. If trust is going to gate what you can do, you deserve to watch it grow.

And a strong graph becomes tradable value. The settlement layer under design lets verified expertise be exposed and compensated across organisational boundaries — your trust graph and proven judgment as an economic asset, settled in VRT, with the open specification stewarded by an independent foundation, deliberately separate from any token operation. Sign in with VAC — the browser as your credential wallet

Designed, and coming with the extension: the product that removes passwords from site-to-site life. Status: design complete, release follows the extension pilot.

Because the paired browser carries your verified credential, any website can request verification and receive your approval in one click from the sidebar. The site gets a completely standard sign-in token — the kind every platform already accepts, with no code changes — while sites that want more can read richer attributes: your verification tier, delegation scope, and audit chain.

For a developer, adding it is one line. The widget handles the ceremony, the extension supplies the credential, and every sign-in is another sealed, auditable event — and another edge in the user's trust graph.

// that's the integration.

What makes this different

A verified human at the root — at the moment of the decision

Most systems verify a person once, at login, and trust everything downstream. Here the authority chain is anchored to a live, biometrically verified human, and that verification is fresh at the point where it matters. Agents and surfaces act on delegated authority they can prove — never authority they assert.

Trust is extended, never re-created — and it compounds

New surfaces don't get accounts; they get vouched for. Every vouch, reference, and sealed decision strengthens a trust graph that belongs to you — visible, portable, and increasingly valuable the more of your digital life runs through it.

Nothing adapts silently

When the system makes a choice on your behalf — a model, a format, a filter — it says so, in plain language, at the moment of the choice, and seals that explanation into the receipt. You're in control because you can always see the controls moving.

Governance rides in the work, not the infrastructure

Each unit of work carries its own policy limits, evidence requirements, and sealed phase records — so correctness doesn't depend on trusting any particular server, and the audit trail is complete by construction.

Built on open standards, extended in the open

The protocol adopts proven standards where they exist, extends them where verified human authority needs expressing, and contributes new specifications through open standards bodies — so no one, including us, can lock you in. See the full map.
