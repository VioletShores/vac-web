A briefing for clinical AI and health technology teams

The AI flagged it. The clinician decided. A sealed record that proves both — and keeps them separate.

A verified clinician, at the moment of the decision — not a log entry reconstructed later.

VAC proves each clinician is present and verified (live biometric), that the AI output was advisory and not the decision, and that a specific, accountable human made the call — a sealed receipt that survives years of scrutiny.

The core accountability gap: when an AI module recommends a clinical action and a clinician acts on it, the record usually captures the outcome — not whether the AI's suggestion was reviewed, whether the clinician formed an independent judgment, or who exactly signed off. That gap matters when a patient outcome is questioned, when Medicare or a health insurer audits, or when a medical board examines whether a registered practitioner made an independent decision or rubber-stamped an AI output.

Why this matters now

Ahpra's position statement on AI in healthcare is explicit: AI tools are assistants; the registered practitioner remains responsible for every clinical decision and must be able to demonstrate independent judgment. That statement was issued without a mechanism for proving it — no standard exists for cryptographically binding a registered clinician's independent decision to an AI advisory output, at the moment the decision was made.

The TGA (Therapeutic Goods Administration) now requires Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) to document clinical decision pathways. And the liability exposure is not theoretical: in a 2025 UK coroner's inquiry, a hospital trust faced scrutiny over whether a clinician's discharge decision was genuinely independent or AI-influenced, with no audit trail distinguishing the AI recommendation from the clinician's documented intent. The inquest ran for three months over a case that a sealed decision receipt would have resolved in an afternoon.

VAC's contribution is narrow and specific: a verified clinician, a decision documented as explicitly independent of the AI suggestion, a sealed cryptographic receipt — and an audit trail that makes the chain of authority answerable, long after the clinical moment has passed.

End-to-end architecture — clinical AI governance

In order, end to end:

  • A patient case arrives — imaging, history, context — and an AI module produces an advisory observation only. The AI output is never the clinical decision.
  • The case routes to a verified, registered clinician. Before the decision is sealed, a biometric step-up confirms a live human is present — not a bot, a recording, a delegated entry, or an AI-generated sign-off.
  • A pre-seal safety checklist of six clinician confirmations is sealed into the same record: that images were reviewed independently, that the AI output was noted but not adopted, that the decision is the clinician's own professional judgment.
  • The clinician selects a decision path (five available, including explicit rejection of the AI suggestion). Both the AI advisory and the clinician's independent decision are sealed in the same record — explicitly separated, never conflated.
  • A sealed, independently-verifiable receipt (Ed25519) records who decided, what was decided, and the explicit separation from the AI advisory — answerable to Ahpra, a Medicare audit, an insurer, or a regulator years later.
