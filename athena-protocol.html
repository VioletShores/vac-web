Architecture · Protocol Detail

The protocol layers — and where they stand with the standards.

Trust infrastructure is only credible if it composes with what the world already runs. Every layer below states plainly which standards it adopts unchanged, which it extends because verified human authority needed a way to be expressed, and which specifications we are creating and contributing through open standards bodies.

Adopt — used as published, unchanged Extend — standard base + verified-authority attributes Create — new specification, contributed openly

Identity & Ceremony

who the human is, provably, right now

The root of everything above it: a live biometric ceremony establishing that a specific human is present and consenting at the moment authority is exercised — not merely that someone once knew a password.

NIST SP 800-63-4adopt

Digital identity assurance tiers; each ceremony event is stamped with the assurance level it achieved.

ISO/IEC 30107-3adopt

Presentation-attack detection for liveness — independently validated components in the biometric stack.

Verified Human Rootcreate

Our Internet-Draft defining an issuance-bound assertion that a live, verified human sits at the root of an authority chain — submitted to the IETF.

Authorization & Delegation

what each surface, site, and agent may do

Authority flows outward from the verified root in explicit, provable grants. Devices pair through standard flows; sites receive standard tokens; agents act inside scopes they can demonstrate, never scopes they assert.

OAuth 2.0 & RFC 8628adopt

The device-authorization pattern behind browser pairing: a short code, approved on an already-trusted surface.

OIDC / SAMLadopt

"Sign in with VAC" issues completely standard tokens, so any existing platform accepts them with zero code change.

GNAP (RFC 9635)extend

Grant negotiation extended with verified-authority attributes — verification tier, delegation scope, audit chain — for platforms that want more than a bare token.

Autonomy Governorcreate

Our Internet-Draft for a pre-action assurance decision: the bound in force at the moment an autonomous action is attempted — submitted to the IETF.

Work & Governance

how a task carries its own rules

Every task runs as a packet through a sealed lifecycle — planned, researched, executed, verified, deployed, confirmed, attested. The packet carries its policy limits, evidence requirements, and sensor contract with it, so correctness never depends on trusting the infrastructure it happens to run on.

EU AI Act alignmentadopt

Packet phases map to the Act's oversight, record-keeping, and post-market-monitoring record classes by construction.

ISO/IEC 42001adopt

AI-management context and risk-management records expressed through the packet's purpose and decision envelopes.

The Packet Formatcreate

The open specification of the governed task lifecycle itself — the format any conformant executor can run — stewarded for open publication through the foundation.

Attestation & Evidence

the record that survives scrutiny

Each phase transition is sealed as a signed, hash-linked statement. The deliberate consequence of building on the standards below: an Athena seal chain is readable by standard verifiers that have never heard of Athena.

COSE (RFC 9052)adopt

Signed structure for every phase seal — not a bespoke signature format.

SCITTadopt

The seal chain follows the transparency-log pattern: append-only signed statements, each carrying its predecessor's hash.

JWS / JWTadopt

Fallback interoperability encoding so receipts verify anywhere the web already verifies things.

SPIFFEadopt

Workload identity for the executor that seals each phase — the machine leg of the chain, grounded by the human root.

Settlement & Value

trust as an economy

When verified expertise crosses an organisational boundary — a custom model invoked, a trust sub-graph consulted, a proven harness rented — the exchange settles in VRT, with the authority chain deciding who may invoke what and the receipt attesting every use.

VRT Settlement Speccreate

v0.1 in draft, published as a foundation specification. Deliberately structured so the standards body stewards the format and never operates the token economy — neutrality is the point.

What we're contributing

Specifications submitted or in draft through open bodies, under the author's name on the public record.

Verified Human Root

draft-zagarella-verified-human-root · IETF Internet-Draft

An assertion format proving a live, biometrically verified human sits at the root of an agent's authority chain — with the four properties agent identity work keeps circling: positional independence, durability, issuance independence, and attribution at the moment of action. The same pattern is designed to extend to embodied systems: a verified robot root — hardware-attested identity and provenance for physical machines — always chaining upward to human authority.

Autonomy Governor

draft-zagarella-autonomy-governor · IETF Internet-Draft

A neutral, pre-action assurance decision profile: before an autonomous system acts, a request/response exchange establishes the bound in force — which human's authority applies, at what tier, with what limits — and seals the answer into the evidence chain.

IETF Agent-Protocol Work

AGENTPROTO & OAuth working groups · active participation

Beyond our own drafts, we contribute the verified-authority perspective inside the IETF's emerging agent-protocol and authorization work — the questions of who an agent acts for, whether that authority is still live, and how it's proven at the moment of action.

The Packet Format & VRT Settlement

Foundation specifications · aip.foundation · in draft

The governed-task lifecycle and the settlement layer for cross-boundary verified expertise, stewarded by an independent Geneva foundation so that adoption never means lock-in — to us or anyone else.

Why this posture matters: institutions don't adopt trust infrastructure a single vendor controls. They adopt standards a neutral body stewards, implemented by whoever earns the work. Building on COSE, SCITT, OAuth, GNAP, and NIST assurance — and contributing our layers back through the same bodies — is how a protocol becomes infrastructure rather than a product.
